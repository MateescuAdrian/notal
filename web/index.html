Fingering Grammar

Why ↑ and ↓ Arrows Sit in Front of Finger Numbers

↑1 • ↑2 • ↑3 • ↑4

Sharps (♯): Little Up Arrow in Front

Whenever a note carries a sharp (♯), Notal writes a sharp-contrast little up arrow in front of the finger number (such as ↑3 for F♯3 on the D string or ↑1 for C♯2 on the C string), signaling a half-step upward extension.

↓1 • ↓2 / ↑1 • ↑2

Flats (♭): Flexible Arrow Convention

For flats (♭), Notal places a downward arrow in front of the finger number by default (e.g. ↓1 for B♭3 on A string or ↓2 for E♭2 on C string), and provides a 1-tap convention toggle so players who prefer ↑ for both ♯ and ♭ can switch instantaneously.

Optical Scan & Auto-Detection

Our horizontal luminance projection detector locks onto the five staff lines, clusters dark elliptical noteheads and left-adjacent accidentals, identifies Bass (𝄢), Tenor (𝄡), or Treble (𝄞) clef range, and maps each pitch to an ergonomic cello string and position.
